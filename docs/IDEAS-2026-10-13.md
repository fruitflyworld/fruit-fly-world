## Ideas that fit the fly (recorded 2026-10-13, not started)

These came out of reading Anthropic's red-team study on models that copy themselves across cheap
devices. The study is a warning; the parts worth borrowing are the *readable* ones. None of them
requires anything to run outside this project's own server.

| Idea | One line | Where it would live | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lineage diary** | A fly dies; its successor inherits a short, sealed diary — "this trade lost money, don't repeat it" — and the diary is part of the record, so the inheritance is replayable | `/fly/[slug]`, `app/lib/market/diary.ts` | small |
| **Reproduction index** | One number per family: how many offspring each generation produces. Above 1 the family grows, below 1 it dies out. Shown as a single large figure | homepage, `/fly/[slug]` | small |
| **Slower rhythm** | One diary entry per day, one race per week, instead of tick-by-tick noise — closer to how a lineage actually reads | site copy, diary frequency | trivial |

The study's own measured effect — notes raising the share of successful runs from 45% in the first
generation to 61–84% later — is Anthropic's result on their setup, not ours, and is cited as such
wherever it appears: <https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai>.

Two ideas from the same reading belong somewhere else entirely and are **not** for this repository:
the "split when you can afford a child" economics, and a defender-side game about pushing a
reproduction number below one. They are separate projects, deliberately unrelated to the fly.
